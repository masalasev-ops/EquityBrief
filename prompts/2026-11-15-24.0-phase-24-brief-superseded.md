What phase 24 is for
The system runs every night on the rules frozen at the second restart of 15 Nov. Phase 24 does three things while that record builds:
- makes code changes safe, so a change that alters no trade never resets a record;
- adds new ingredients, as background tests;
- builds an AI judge, last.
The rule freeze binds the whole phase.
- No live rule's buy, stop, take-profit, sale or market check changes before the monthly run of Saturday 6 Feb 2027.
- Everything new registers as a background version, or as a setup on test with its own record. Nothing new goes live before that run, and then only on my word.
- Tonight's picks carry on every night, unchanged.
Every pick from any new setup or account gives a buy price, a stop and a price to take profit.
Each checkpoint ends with something I can open and look at, and adds its expectations to the fixture. One track at a time, in the order below.

Part 1: safe changes and the freeze's own work. This comes first, because everything after it moves code.
1. Records survive harmless code changes.
   - The replay that keeps a record when every trade reproduces covers every kind of rule: the family rules on all three indexes, the swing filter's versions, the ladder rules, the candidates and the books. It reads each night's inputs as that night stored them.
   - A pin move whose replay reproduces every trade keeps the record.
   - During the freeze, register refuses to restart a live rule's record, so register --moved can pass the freeze check.
   - The Run page shows each rule's replay result after a pin move.
   - Any kind of rule the replay cannot cover is named, with the reason.
2. The stale share-count figure fixed in the history builds, the monthly tests and the point-in-time check, with a count of the rows it moved. This lands before the 5 Dec monthly run.
3. A full practice monthly run on a copy of the restarted store before 5 Dec, reporting its proposals for each family on each index. Then the 5 Dec and 2 Jan runs report as usual, and nothing goes live from them.
4. The books' own exit, with no stop and no take-profit, registered as background versions on all nine books. Bring me the choice of which version to retire in Quality and Fundamentals, which are full.
5. The pullbacks' search over the 20-year history, the S&P 500's first, since it does worst.
   - Show me the registered list of settings, the number of versions and the run time before it runs.
   - Choose on 2005 to 2018 and judge from 2019, against the benchmark.
   - Say plainly whether any version beats the index's other stocks bought the same way.
   - Versions that do better run as background versions.
6. The 6 Feb decision page, built in December so I can watch it fill week by week.
   - For each live rule: its record since the restart of 15 Nov, and beside it each background version's record. Per $1 risked for the families, points a month for the books.
   - One plain line saying whether each is beating stocks picked at random.
   - The books' own exits shown beside their new exits.
   - The rule for 6 Feb, printed on the page and registered now, before any results: a background version replaces a live rule only if all three hold:
     - over the weeks since 15 Nov it beat the live rule;
     - its 20-year test also beat the live rule's;
     - it closed at least the rules' minimum count of trades.
     Otherwise the live rule stays.

Part 3: the AI judge, last, from the plan of 2 Nov
9. A mockup on the site's real theme first, in the prompts folder, before any build.
10. The judge itself.
   - The ruling that no model judges a stock changes to allow this one separate account and nothing else.
   - How it picks: each night my local model picks its own stocks from all 1,500, in two rounds.
     - First round: code writes a short fact row for each stock, with names, tickers and dates removed and sizes as bands. The model screens them about 50 at a time and keeps about 30.
     - Second round: it reads a full fact sheet for each and picks up to 5, ranked, each with a short reason.
     - It runs in the local model's slot and is stored before the open.
   - Its plan: the model chooses from a menu of holding periods and exits. Code sets every price from the stock's typical daily move, and every pick takes profit at a price.
   - A fair comparison: four accounts with the same money, sizing and costs:
     - the rules;
     - the AI judge;
     - random picks from all 1,500 with the same plan menu;
     - holding the index.
   - The page:
     - one chart of the four accounts;
     - a scoreboard: trades, winners, result per $1 risked, gain over the comparison, and whether it beats random so far;
     - "where the AI looked": its picks no setup found, picks it shared with the rules, and rule picks it passed over, each with results;
     - the AI's reason on each pick card;
     - one line on Home.
   - Hindsight:
     - Only nights after the model's training cutoff are replayed, and they are labelled replayed.
     - A test checks that the model cannot name the anonymised stocks.
     - The live record from its start is what counts.
   - Cost: the local model only. No paid model without my approval and a named amount.
   - It never sets Tonight's buys unless a monthly run and my word adopt it, and not before 6 Feb.
