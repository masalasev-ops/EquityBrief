## The phase in plain words

**What it does.** Three things while the frozen rules build their record: it makes code changes safe, it adds new ingredients as background tests, and last it builds an AI judge in an account of its own.

**What goes wrong today.** Three faults, each read off the code.
- A harmless edit throws records away. The replay that keeps a record reads only the S&P 500's breakout and drift, and refuses any rule that trades a plan or reads the ledger. Since the restart nearly every rule trades a plan, so today the replay would keep almost no record at all.
- Nothing but the cards' wording and one check over remedy files knows about the freeze. An approval pressed on the Monthly check-up page on 5 Dec would still register a live rule again at the change.
- The ledger's history builds work a stock's share-count check with the first session that asked, so a later session can read a stale figure.

**What changes.** A replay for every kind of rule, and `register` refusing to restart a live record during the freeze. The share figure fixed. A practice monthly run before 5 Dec. Then the books' own exits, the pullbacks' search and the 6 Feb decision page. Then the earnings premium and four new readings, all as tests. Then the AI judge.

**Example.** Adding one reading to the ledger's catalogue edits a file that the dip's, Quality's, the Fundamentals family's and the S&P 500 heavyweights' rules all pin. Today all of those records would restart, though no trade changes. After 24.1 the replay re-runs each rule over every night since 15 Nov, finds every trade the same, and each record carries on. If one trade differs on a live rule, `register` writes nothing and the change comes to you.
