## The phase in plain words

**What the system does today.** An idea reaches a test in two ways: once a month through three engines (better exits from a fixed menu of 30, one extra condition, a learned order), or through a build session. The local model proposes at most three conditions a family a month. Nothing remembers a failed idea in one place. One line on some pick cards says what similar past setups made. A version that passes waits for your approval even to run in the background.

**What goes wrong.** You are the bottleneck for ideas, and the loop cannot say an idea richer than "one exit" or "one condition". Nobody can weigh 20 years and 1,500 stocks by eye, and the system does not do it for you.

**What changes.** The fixed AI judge is built first, so it picks live as soon as it is ready and its record builds while the rest is built. Then one loop:
- every week, the local model reads each rule's failure list and a notebook of every idea ever tested, and proposes new ideas in a closed grammar;
- code tests each idea the same way: chosen on 2005 to 2018, judged from 2019, with a correction for luck that counts every idea;
- an idea that passes runs from that night by itself, since it changes no live rule;
- every night, code compares each pick with the most similar past cases and with random picks;
- at a monthly run from 6 Feb 2027, a background version that has earned it gets a case you can read in two minutes, and two buttons.

**Example.** The S&P 600 pullback's losers mostly had their sector under its 20-day average. The researcher proposes: "the S&P 600 pullback, only where its sector is above its 20-day average; stop 1.5 typical days under the buy; take profit on half at 2 times the risk; sell after 20 sessions." Code tests it and it clears the week's bar, so it runs from that night. Once it has closed 50 trades and beaten the live rule over the same nights, the next monthly run draws its case and you click promote, or keep.

## The correction for luck as ideas grow, and how many a week

**Today.** Proposals for one rule in one run are read together (a step-down over shared sign flips of 63-session blocks). Across runs the bar is fixed: 0.05 over 12 runs a year, 1 in 240.

**From the first weekly run.**
- Every idea tested in a week, a book's among them, is read together in one step-down, so ten ideas share one bar and ideas that rise and fall together are not charged twice.
- Each week's bar is 0.05 over 52 weeks: 1 in 1,040. Over a year of weekly runs the chance that even one survivor is luck stays about 5%, however many ideas were tested.
- The level is a year's, not a lifetime's, on your word of today. Over five years the chance of at least one lucky survivor running in the background is about 23%.
- An idea with settings to choose (the pullbacks' search) counts once: only the setting chosen on 2005 to 2018 is ever read against the late years.
- The late years (2019 to 2026-11-13) only admit an idea to the background. The proof is live nights that did not exist when the idea was proposed: 50 closed trades over the same nights, and your click.
- Each case's "argument against" prints the counts: ideas tested to date, how many passed, and how many a year of luck alone would pass.

**What a larger week costs.** Worst case, as if no two ideas overlapped:

|---|---|---|

| 3 | 1 in 3,120 | 1.22 times |

**What I did not rest it on.** Online false discovery rate methods (LORD++, SAFFRON) let the bar ease after real finds and suit an endless stream, but their guarantees are proved for independent tests or a narrow kind of dependence, and our ideas all share one history.
