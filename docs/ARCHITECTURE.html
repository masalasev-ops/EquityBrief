1. Purpose and boundaries

What it is for. To select, every evening, the stocks worth buying or paying attention to as of that day, and then to produce a full research report on any name you choose to open. The operator's goal is that tonight's list holds the best stocks at a buy point for a swing trade of a few weeks, and that any name in the index still opens to its report. The list is chosen by the swing filter's gates, each a fact about the chart or the plan, and the improvement loop in section 13 exists to make it select better. (see: Tonight's list selects the stocks worth buying or paying attention to that evening, and the improvement loop exists to make that selection better) (see: Tonight's list is the swing filter's, and an evening is listed by the rule that listed it) Four of the six conditions detect arrival at a price the evening's arithmetic has an instruction for, one detects a break on unusual volume, and one is a calendar fact, and the six are computed for every name every night and stand beside the list as context. The arithmetic the gates read is not authored in advance and is not carried over from a previous night: the levels and the plan are recomputed from the chart every evening, so a name arriving at a level and the plan naming that level are the same night's work. Whether a listed name's setup succeeds is measured by the loop over time rather than asserted, and section 13 is how. The universe is the S&P 500, and its membership is fetched from the provider rather than maintained by hand. (see: The universe is the S&P 500, and membership is fetched, not maintained) The report it produces, specified in section 4, (see: Section 4 of the architecture defines the report region by region, and nothing outside the corpus does) carries a narrative verdict, how the stock got here with the causes of its biggest moves, what the company sells, the numbers over five quarters, the industry cycle it sits in, the bull and bear cases side by side, a chart with levels and a volume profile, a staged entry and exit plan in two books, a risk table where every risk names the number that would confirm it, and a dated calendar.

The constraint that shapes everything. Nightly work must be free and fast, and paid work must happen only when you ask for it. So the nightly run is arithmetic over price bars with no model calls and no per-name network calls, and everything expensive happens when you open a name and is then stored permanently with the date it was written.

What it is not. The universe is a published index membership, not a filtered set, and tonight's list is chosen on the chart and the plan alone, with no fundamentals and no model in the decision. (see: No reading of the fundamentals fires a reason, gates a tranche or draws a panel) It does not trade, does not size positions, and produces no short or options ideas. (see: The plan places a position and never sizes one)

Who runs it. Program in .NET, with a local model on and an EODHD subscription.

2. The shape in one paragraph

After the US close, one bulk call fetches the day's bars for the whole exchange. For all 500 names the system computes indicators, swings, a volume profile, level bands, a trend label, an entry and exit ladder, the six reasons as context, and each name's answer at the swing filter's five gates. That is seconds of arithmetic and it costs nothing. The result is stored. When you open the app you see tonight's list, the names the swing filter passed, in its order (see: Tonight's list is the swing filter's, and an evening is listed by the rule that listed it). When you open a name, the computed sections of its report appear immediately from the store, its fundamentals fill from the provider, and if no research record exists for it a research pass runs once, reads filings and news, writes the narrative sections, and stores them with an as-of date and every source it read. Opening that name again shows the stored research with its date. A new pass runs only when a filing appears, an earnings date passes, the name's news feed moves materially, or you press refresh. (see: Research is a stored document with an as-of date, not a cache)

3. Vocabulary

Every specialist word used later in this document. Market words first, then the project's own words.

Moving averageThe average closing price over the last n days, recalculated daily. It smooths noise. Price above a rising average is the simplest definition of an uptrend.

Fibonacci retracementFixed fractions (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) of the distance between a swing high and a swing low. Widely watched, so bounces often stall there.
Volume profile, volume at priceHow many shares changed hands in each price band over a period. Where a lot traded, many holders have that price as their cost, so they defend it on the way down and sell at breakeven on the way up. Where little traded, price moves through quickly.
ATR, average true rangeHow far the stock moves on a normal day, averaged over 14 days. The report calls it "a typical day's move". It sets how far a stop must sit so ordinary noise does not trigger it.
RSIA momentum score from 0 to 100. Near 50 is balanced, above 70 stretched upward, below 30 stretched downward.
MACDThe gap between a fast and a slow average, with a signal line. A shrinking positive histogram means a rally is losing pace rather than reversing.
Trend stateA one-word label for the chart, uptrend, downtrend or range. It decides which ladder shape applies and how stops are placed.
TrancheOne step of a staged purchase. The plan buys at successively lower support bands rather than all at once.
StopThe price at which an entry is admitted to be wrong. This system uses daily-close stops: the exit triggers only if the day ends beyond the line, because intraday dips through a level are routine.
InvalidationThe single price below which the whole position, not just one tranche, is wrong.
Reward to riskDistance from entry to target divided by distance from entry to stop, which on the swing filter's list is read from the swing plan entered at the night's close.
The print, guidanceThe quarterly results announcement, and the company's own forecast for the next quarter given with it. Stocks move most on those days.
Base rateWhat happens to an average name on an average night. Any claim that a signal predicts something must beat it, because most stocks rise over a month regardless.
Break-evenThe share of the time a plan must reach its target before its stop simply to come out level. It is arithmetic on the plan's own numbers: risk divided by risk plus reward. A plan with a distant target and a tight stop needs to be right rarely; one with a near target and a wide stop needs to be right often.
Computed sectionA part of the report produced by arithmetic over stored bars and provider data. No model is involved and every number is reproducible.
Researched sectionA part of the report produced by a model reading filings, news and theme material. Written once, stored with an as-of date, and never rewritten on a timer.
Facts fileThe JSON for one name on one night holding every number the computed sections may use, each with its source. It is what the model is allowed to see and what the fixture is diffed against.
Research recordThe stored output of one research pass for one name: the prose of each researched section, the as-of date, the model used, and every source document read.
Theme recordResearch about an industry rather than a company, for example memory-chip contract pricing. One record serves every name in that industry.
Tonight's listThe names the swing filter passed tonight, drawn in its order with no fixed length, and on an evening before the switch the names where one of the six reasons fired.
ReasonOne of the six stated conditions, which chose tonight's list before the swing filter did and stand beside its names as context from it.
ListingThe record of one member on one night, holding which reasons fired and the plan as it stood that night, written for every member every night whether or not the evening's rule listed it and kept forever (see: Your own listing history is kept forever) (see: Tonight's list is the swing filter's, and an evening is listed by the rule that listed it).
SetupOne listing considered as a prediction: an entry, a stop and a target that will eventually be right or wrong. A setup starts on the first close at or below its entry zone's top edge and is resolved when the target is reached, the stop is closed through, or the time cap expires; a target reached before it starts is never entered, and never a win.
ShadowScored and stored exactly like a live thing, but shown nowhere and acting on nothing. A shadow condition accumulates a record of how it would have done, so a decision to adopt it later rests on evidence rather than on the idea.
FixtureA frozen, committed set of inputs with expected outputs beside them, so a run is checked by diff rather than by opinion.
HarnessThe one command that reads this document's tables, checks the code against them, runs the fixture, and reports every claim as PASS, FAIL or UNEXAMINED.
BlockFor judging a candidate, a run of 63 consecutive trading sessions keyed on the session its setup was read on, the unit its test counts because setups inside one block can share the bars they resolve over.
LookOne of the few points, fixed when a candidate is registered, at which its verdict is read: at 8, 12 and 16 non-empty blocks. Between looks the verdict does not move.
Information fractionHow far a candidate is through the evidence it was registered to gather, the blocks it holds over the blocks at its last look. The looks sit at 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0.
Null win probabilityThe chance a setup with no edge reaches its target before its stop, simulated from its own plan, costs and the name's volatility. It replaces the planned break-even as the bar a setup is scored against.
Design effectHow much the clustering of setups inflates the variance of a record over what the same number of independent setups would give. At 2, a record of 250 carries the information of 125.
Sign-flip testThe test a candidate is judged by: each block's excess of wins over null probabilities is flipped in sign in every combination, and the p-value is the share of combinations at least as extreme as the one observed.
Futility guidelineA rule, read only at the first two looks, that retires a candidate whose record is running below its bar. It binds nothing: the promotion boundary is computed as if it did not exist.
Monitoring figureThe running figure the run page shows every night for a candidate, labelled so. It is not the verdict, which only a look changes.
Closed windowA setup whose whole outcome window has passed, so its result can no longer change. A look counts only these.
Swing filterThe five gates, the trigger's arrival and the exclusions that choose tonight's list, each a stated condition read off the chart and the plan.
GateOne question the swing filter asks of every member each night, in order the market, the name's trend and strength, a setup, a trigger and a trade.
BreadthThe share of the index's members closing above their own 200-day average, which the market gate reads against its floor.
Relative strengthA member's place among the index's members by its return over 63 and over 126 sessions, the mean of the two places.
Pullback, depthHow far the close sits below the highest high of the last 20 sessions, counted in typical days' moves.
Dry-upThe median volume of the sessions since that high against the fifty-day average, which a pullback wants low.
Tight base, tightnessA range whose last ten sessions move less than its last fifty, the base a breakout leaves from.
BreakoutA close above a band that sat at or above the previous close, on volume above its multiple of the fifty-day average.
Trigger, arrivalThe setup's event, a close above the previous session's high or back inside its band, which arrives when it first happens inside the arrival window.
Swing planThe trade the swing filter reads, entered at the night's close, stopped below the setup band's low edge and targeting the nearest resistance band above.
ExclusionA reason a name passing every gate is still left off the list, being earnings inside the holding window, a suspect series or a gap.
Filter versionOne set of the swing filter's settings, opened by your shape command and named on every row a night stores under it.
Shape clockThe count of ordinary nights under one filter version that proposes a setting once a gate's median count sits outside its band.
Event night, ordinary nightA night one cause pushes a usually quiet gate or reason far past its own normal share is an event night that no median reads, and every other night is ordinary.
Gate bandThe range each gate's median count through the funnel is held against, a third to three times what the ruled filter passed.
Edge clockThe swing family's records read at looks over blocks, which can retire a candidate at its first look and promote one no earlier than its second.
Swing familyThe live filter and five variants, each a whole rule with one setting on its other side, judged together as one family of six.
Near missA setup one gate or one exclusion alone rejected while everything else passed, read against its own break-even and null.
List ruleThe rule an evening's list was drawn by, the six reasons before the switch and the swing filter from it, which every surface names.

6. The layers, one at a time

6.1 Sources outside

Prices, index membership, company financials, the earnings calendar and news all come from EODHD, which is already subscribed. Bars arrive through the bulk end-of-day endpoint, which returns the whole exchange in one request, so the nightly fetch does not grow with the size of the universe. Index membership comes from the index constituents endpoint, which carries join and leave dates, so membership is fetched rather than maintained by hand (see: The universe is the S&P 500, and membership is fetched, not maintained). News arrives through one dated query for the whole exchange, paged until the day is covered, and is attributed to names in code rather than asked for per name (see: News is one dated query, paged to cover the day, and attributed to names locally).

Filings come from the regulatory archive, which is free, needs no key, and returns reported financials as structured data. Three things are taken from it. Segment revenue and margin by business unit, which the segment commentary and the numbers' segment table in section 4 need. Management's own guidance, published as the earnings press release exhibit to a results announcement rather than as structured data. And, where a company chooses to file one, the verbatim call transcript as a second exhibit to the same filing.

That third item is opportunistic and the design does not depend on it. Some companies file a transcript and most do not, so the two-cases section is written from the press release and the filings for most names and gains the analysts' questions for the few that file them. No transcript provider is required, and the report omits what it does not have rather than guessing. (see: Transcripts are opportunistic, never a dependency)

Two models are used for different jobs. A local model on the operator's GPU writes prose from numbers, which is a small job it does well and free. A hosted model does research, which is a large-context job a local model does badly. Which hosted model is configuration and not code: the wire format, the address, the model, the options it is asked with and the rates it is priced at are settings, the shipped configuration names DeepSeek V4, and switching model changes those settings and nothing else. The report footer always says which model wrote what, with the options it was asked with. (see: Two models for two jobs, and which research model answers is configuration) (see: The research model is named only in configuration, and a call is priced at the configured rates its own timestamp falls in) (see: A research record is written and dated per section, not as a whole)

One consequence shapes the research runner. The research model is given no web search of its own, whichever provider serves it, so the model cannot go and find things; it can only read what it is handed through tool calls (see: The model never fetches; components fetch and hand it documents). For a per-name pass that costs nothing, because the documents it needs are filings and news the system already fetches. For a theme record it matters, because an industry's own pricing cycle is published by research firms rather than filed with a regulator, and that is section 22's second question.

The shipped provider's rates carry a peak and off-peak split, with peak at double, and the configuration states both. In Eastern time the peak windows fall late at night, so the market session and the hours just after the close are off-peak and interactive reading is never billed at the peak rate. Anything queued rather than interactive is scheduled into off-peak deliberately (see: Queued work runs off-peak, and every schedule is written in UTC). A queued pass starts only where the longest pass the store holds would end before the next peak window opens, because a pass's paid calls come at its end, so the night's own report, asked after the overnight queue, can wait until the window ends (see: A pass starts only where the longest pass the store holds would end before a peak window opens).

6.2 Store owned

One SQLite file. One year of bars is kept per name and no more (see: One year of bars, and no more). One year for 500 names is about 10 MB, and the whole store including research is comfortably under a gigabyte, so it is copied into a fixture, diffed and backed up by hand (see: The whole system is a checkout and one database file). Section 16 lists the tables.

6.3 Compute deterministic, nightly

This layer holds the stages that compute and the fetchers that feed them, and the property the fixture rests on belongs to the first group. Every stage that computes is a pure function of stored data: given the same bars it produces the same facts file byte for byte, which is what makes the fixture diff possible. The fetchers are not pure and nothing but this sentence ever said they were, which it did by calling every component in the layer pure and giving a count of eleven that the catalogue passed during phase 4 and that nothing read. A fetcher's property is a different one and is asserted elsewhere: one request for the whole index whatever its size, every page counted, and a payload refused rather than stored where it is not the answer that was asked for. The four carrying the most logic are opened up in sections 8 to 11.

6.4 Research on demand, paid

A pass runs for one name when it is opened and no valid record exists. It reads that name's filings and recent news, reads or triggers its theme record, and writes the narrative sections. Everything it read is stored with the record. The claim checker then rejects numbers not present in the facts file and claims whose source is not in the store. What survives is written once and read from then on.

6.5 Serve pages

A local web application serves a read-only API over the store and hosts the single-page app. Reports are rendered in the browser from stored data rather than written to disk nightly, so a layout change costs nothing and nothing goes stale on disk. The exporter renders one name through the same code path into a self-contained file, because handing a single report to someone is a stated use. (see: A single report can still be exported as a self-contained file)

6.6 Check sign-off

The harness is what makes a phase finished. It has no opinion of its own; it reads the claims this document makes and tests them. Section 19.

8. What the three builders each do, and why there are three

All three run for every name in the index, every night. None of them is watch-list only, and none is triggered by opening a name. They are three stages of one chain, each answering a different question about a single stock.

Key. The dashed box is the whole nightly chain for one stock, repeated for every name in the index. Green boxes compute something that stays true until the chart changes. The orange box is the only one of the three that asks a question about today. At the bottom, the blue-topped box on the left is what every name gets regardless, and the orange-topped box on the right is the small set you actually read, which the swing filter chooses from what the three leave behind (see: Tonight's list is the swing filter's, and an evening is listed by the rule that listed it).

The arrows run one way only. The shortlist cannot be built without the ladder, and the ladder cannot be built without the bands, which is why they are three components rather than one.

8.1 The worked example

One name's figures are used throughout this document to make rules concrete. They come from a year of that name's daily bars, a large-capitalisation semiconductor company trading around 933 at the time, and they are cited later wherever a rule needs a number to be legible. The worked example illustrates the rules; it never defines them. Where a rule and the worked example disagree, the rule is right and the example is restated.

The Level builder reads sixty sessions of that stock's bars and finds the prices that matter. In the worked example those are support bands at 900 to 940, 855 to 870, 818 to 830 and 738 to 770, and resistance at 996, at 1057, at 1144 to 1214, and at the all-time high. Its output is a list of price bands, each with a role and the evidence that makes it a band. It knows nothing about buying or selling.

The Ladder builder takes those bands and turns them into a plan. The first tranche sits on the 900 to 940 band and stops on a daily close below 855, which is the low edge of the band beneath it. The second sits on 855 to 870 and stops below 815. The third sits on 818 to 830 and stops below 737. Exits scale out from 1057 upward, and the band at 996 is listed but not traded because it is closer than two typical days' moves to the blended entry, which is the mean of the first two tranche zones' midpoints and the price the position is carried at once the plan has staged what it can. None of this is about tonight; the plan holds for as long as the bands do.

The Shortlist builder is the only one of the three that asks about today. It takes the plan and tonight's close and tests six conditions. With the close at 933, the price is inside a tranche zone, which here is the first, so the reason named at entry zone fires. The next earnings date is inside the twenty-session horizon, so earnings soon fires as well. Whether the name is on tonight's list is the swing filter's answer, read through its five gates over the same plan and bands (section 11.2), and where it is, its row shows the two reasons beside the name as context (see: Tonight's list is the swing filter's, and an evening is listed by the rule that listed it).

Now take a name whose close sits in the middle of its range, far from every band, with no earnings near and ordinary volume. Nothing fires, and with no band holding its close the swing filter finds no setup, so it is not on the list. Its bands and its plan were still computed and stored, so opening it deliberately still shows a complete chart, a level table and a ladder. That is the difference between the two boxes at the foot of the figure: the left one is produced for all five hundred names, the right one is the handful worth reading tonight.

8.2 Why three components rather than one

The dependency runs strictly one way, and each stage has a different failure mode. A wrong band produces a wrong plan for that name. A wrong plan produces a wrong list row. A wrong condition threshold produces a list of the wrong length while every band and plan underneath it stays correct, which is a completely different thing to diagnose.

Keeping them apart is also what makes the read and write matrix in section 16 able to say anything. Each builder declares what it may touch, so a defect in the ladder cannot corrupt the bands the chart draws from, and the fixture can catch each stage on its own rather than only catching the end of the chain.

Touches are why a band can cite specific dated lows inside it without those days being swings in their own right. A touch is a visit rather than a session, because how long the price sat in a band is not how many times the band held it: a band the price entered, stayed in for three weeks and left has one touch, not fifteen. A visit holding a session the band already has as a swing or a retracement adds nothing, since that session is in the band once already.

The volume shelf is the addition this version makes, and it is load bearing rather than decorative. Where a large share of a period's volume changed hands in a narrow price range, many holders have that price as their cost, so they defend it on the way down and sell at breakeven on the way up. Where very little traded, there is nobody with a reason to act and price moves through quickly. A level builder without a volume profile can state neither, and both statements belong in the report. The five retracements are drawn between the window's most recent swing high and its most recent swing low, in whichever order they occurred. Such a band is still a level and still appears on the chart, because a reader wants to see where the average sits, but each band records whether anything other than a moving average anchors it. 10.2 Why the entry is a zone, and what the midpoint is for

A band is not a line, and the first step of a plan is not a price. The level builder reads sixty sessions of a name's bars and collects every candidate a level can come from: each swing high and low, the 20, 50 and 200-day averages, the five retracements between the last swing high and the last swing low, and every price range holding an unusually large share of the period's volume. It sorts them by price and merges, so that two candidates closer than half a typical day's move become one band whose edges are its lowest and highest member, which on the worked example is a support band running from 900 to 940. The ladder builder makes that band the first tranche because its low edge is below the price and something other than a moving average anchors it, and it keeps the band's whole width although tonight's close of 933 sits inside it, since a band the price is already trading in is exactly the band the first step belongs on. The stop is a daily close below 855, the low edge of the band beneath. The shortlist builder then asks whether tonight's close is inside a tranche zone; at 933 it is, so at entry zone fires. Every step of that chain is stated in section 9 and above in section 10, and what this subsection adds is what becomes of the zone at the point the arithmetic needs one price. Evidence: Park and Irwin (2007) review 95 modern studies of technical trading and count 56 positive, 20 negative and 19 mixed, and name data snooping, the selection of rules after the fact and the estimation of risk and transaction costs as problems most of those studies carry, so a count of positive results is not itself a finding. Not settled by research: whether a price level predicts anything at all, which is the premise the whole chain rests on and on which Osler (2000) and Chung and Bellotti (2021) already disagree (the standing of levels).

A fill can land anywhere between a band's edges, so the risk arithmetic has to be carried at one price, and the midpoint is the only price in the band that is not a choice. Risk per tranche is measured from the midpoint to the stop and reward from the midpoint to the first traded exit, which on the worked example is 65 points against 137 computed at 920, and the reward to risk on the page is that ratio. It is stated twice, from the first tranche and from the blended first two, because a plan staged across two zones is carried at neither of them alone, and the blended entry is also what the skipped exit is measured from. Judgement: the midpoint rather than the low edge, the high edge or the close. A low edge assumes the best fill the band allows and a high edge the worst, so each is a forecast of the fill wearing the clothes of arithmetic, and the midpoint is the one point in the band that asserts nothing about where the fill landed.

No record is scored from the midpoint. A setup's outcome is decided from the close of the session it was listed on, admitted only where that close sits inside the plan's own range, and the break-even stored beside the outcome is measured from that close (see: A stored break-even is measured from the close the setup was entered at, as a percentage beside the figures it is compared with) (see: An entry is admitted only inside the plan's own range, and a plan with no entry zone takes its whole range as its zone). So the figure section 13.1 states for the worked example is what its plan demanded on the evening it was drawn, and the bar a condition's record is judged against is the mean of the break-evens its own setups stored. Reading the first as the second is reading a planned figure as a measured one. Judgement: keeping the two apart rather than scoring from the midpoint, because a record scored at a price no order was ever placed at would measure how the plan was drawn instead of how it turned out.

A plan places up to three tranches rather than taking the whole position at the first, which stages a purchase instead of making it at once. Evidence: Brennan, Li and Torous (2005), over securities and dates drawn at random from CRSP between 1926 and 2003, compare the certainty equivalent of staging against buying outright for an investor with iso-elastic utility. For one who already holds the market portfolio and buys a marginal amount of a further stock, staging dominates buying outright for execution periods up to 48 months, and at 36 months the advantage runs from 5% to 134% according to risk aversion, to whether the portfolio held is value or equal weighted, and to how the added stock is drawn; where the initial portfolio is optimally levered the advantage narrows to about 3% to 6% and, on an equal weighted portfolio, disappears or reverses. For buying a diversified portfolio they find only mixed support, because a constant half in stocks and half in cash rebalanced monthly beats both. Constantinides (1979) argues the other way, that a schedule fixed in advance cannot use what later periods reveal and so is dominated by strategies that adapt to them. Judgement: what carries over and what does not. Both papers stage over calendar time, in months and in years, while the tranches here stage over price on a plan that stands for as long as the bands do; neither tests entering at a support band, and neither is a measurement of this system. What Brennan, Li and Torous reach by simulation, that transient departures of prices from fundamental value can produce the advantage they measure, is a form of the reversion a support band presumes, which is why their result is read here as a prior and never as a verdict.

The midpoint is not a forecast of where a purchase staged across the zone fills, and the error is not random. How deep into the band the purchase fills and whether the band holds are decided by the same move: a plan filled at the low edge is a plan whose level is under pressure, and one filled only near the high edge is one that turned early. The entry price and the outcome are therefore dependent, and the ratio the page states is computed as though they were not. Evidence: Linnainmaa (2010), over the exchange's order book and the investor-level records of every individual investor in Finland, finds that price-contingent orders execute selectively, and that patterns which read as behavioural, being losses around earnings announcements, weak returns after a trade and contrarian trading, arise mechanically from that selection rather than from the biases they resemble. Judgement: the direction, which is that a better fill price is bought at the cost of worse odds attached to it, and the size, which nothing read here measures over these bands. The system does not assume it away: each setup is judged against a bar simulated from its own plan under its own volatility with costs, and realized loss in multiples of planned risk is reported beside the planned figure, both of them in section 13.6.

13. How the picks improve

Measuring something is not the same as improving it, and until this section the document only measured. This is the loop. It is deliberately small: one score per condition, one register of candidates, and one column in a table that already exists. No new component runs nightly, no new store is needed beyond what the listings already hold, and none of it is built before the evidence exists to run it.

13.1 The score, and why it is not a return

Every listing already carries a plan, and a plan states an entry, a stop and a target. That makes each listing a falsifiable prediction with a bar it must clear, computed from its own arithmetic and nothing else.

Take the worked example's first tranche. Entry near 920, stop on a close below 855, first traded target at 1057. Risk is 65 points, reward is 137, so the setup breaks even if the target is reached before the stop about 32% of the time. That number is not a preference or a benchmark borrowed from elsewhere; it falls out of where the bands sit.

The 920 in that arithmetic is the midpoint of the entry zone. It is where the plan's own figures are computed and it is not where any record is scored. A setup is scored from the close of the session it was listed on, admitted only where that close sits inside the plan's own range, and the break-even stored beside its outcome is measured from that close rather than from the midpoint (see: A stored break-even is measured from the close the setup was entered at, as a percentage beside the figures it is compared with) (see: An entry is admitted only inside the plan's own range, and a plan with no entry zone takes its whole range as its zone). So a plan states one break-even and each of its setups stores another, and the two are different numbers about different entries: the first is what the plan demanded on the evening it was drawn, and the second is the bar that setup is judged against. Section 10 says what the midpoint is for and what it is never used for.

So the question asked of every condition is: across all the setups it produced, did the target come before the stop more often than the mean of the break-evens stored with them required. That is one number, it is a count of stored rows, and it is meaningful in a way a forward return is not. Twenty-one-day returns mostly measure whether the market went up, which is why the run page shows them against the universe base rate. This score has no such problem, because the bar moves with the setup. (see: A condition is judged against the break-even its own plan demands)

Key. Green boxes are computed nightly from stored bars, and the grey boxes at the right of the row are the cases that matter most: a setup whose price never came back to its entry and a setup that has done nothing yet are neither wins nor losses. Counting either as one would flatter or punish a condition for a trade that did not happen. A setup starts on the first close at or below its entry zone's top edge and is held open until the target hits, the stop hits, or the time cap expires. A target reached before that close is never entered, and a timed-out setup is reported separately, and neither is ever a win (see: A setup is scored from its entry, and a target reached before the entry is never a win).

The bar in the second box is computed from the plan itself. A setup with a distant target and a tight stop needs to be right rarely; one with a near target and a wide stop needs to be right often. Comparing conditions on wins alone would reward the second kind for nothing, which is why the score is always relative to what each setup demanded. (see: The significance threshold is divided by the family size, and the divisor is shown)

13.3 The guardrails

The danger in a loop like this is not that it fails to find anything. It is that it finds something that is not there. Run five candidate conditions of no real value and the chance that at least one clears an ordinary significance test is about 23%; run twenty and it is 64%. Picking the best of twenty noise processes and shipping it is the standard way a self-improving system makes itself worse while appearing to learn.

13.4 What each phase owes

The loop is a phase 8 feature. But it cannot be added in phase 8 unless phase 5 stores the right things, because the evidence takes months to accumulate and cannot be recreated afterwards.

The bars can be replayed but the plan cannot, because by the time phase 8 arrives the rules may have changed and recomputing would score old listings under new rules. 13.6 How a candidate is judged

Each setup carries its own bar, as section 13.1 says, and the bar is its null win probability: the chance a plan with no edge reaches its target before its stop, simulated from the plan under the name's trailing volatility with the 63-session cap and a round trip of 10 basis points, with the planned break-even shown beside it (see: A setup's null win probability is calibrated from its own plan, and its planned break-even is shown beside it). 13.7 What the judging can and cannot show

A single read at 250 independent setups, at a 35% break-even and one-sided 0.05 over three, needs a 9.45-point excess for 80% power and has 23.8% power against 4.6 points, the edge published levels showed over arbitrary ones, and 10.2% against 2.9, the edge left five days on if the arbitrary baseline held; at design effects of 1.25 to 3 the 80% figure rises to 10.72 to 16.00 points (see: Power is stated for the looks that exist as well as for a single read). Evidence: Osler (2000): published levels bounced 60.8% of the time against 56.2% for arbitrary ones, and five days after publication 1.7 points less often than on the day. Judgement: the looks that exist hold far more setups than 250: simulated with the level split conservatively between the second and third looks and the edge steady across blocks, arrived and narrow at about 161 setups a block detects a 4.6-point excess 98% of the time at design effect 1 and 65% at 3, and the others 92% to 100%; the first look has no power by construction, heterogeneity across blocks would lower every figure, and each is re-derived on the calibrated null before registration and re-read at every look.

Before the first nightly run, once

The computations from the indicators to the facts file were one step until 4.0, reading "For every name: indicators, swings, volume profile, levels, trend state, ladder, moves, list reasons, facts file". Written as one step the order was invisible and so was the fact that three of the stages, having shipped in phase 3, were not run by any night at all. The trend state and the ladder are one stage rather than two, because the classifier writes nothing and hands its label to the builder that writes the row it sits on.

A feed that answers in pages counts every page it fetched, so a page count that grows with the index is a per-name call whatever the request count says (see: A feed that pages counts every page, and a page count that grows with the index is a per-name call). The run is idempotent in what it records about the market: running it twice on one night produces the same bars, the same membership spans and the same computed values, and where a second run computes different values than the first, because a refetch moved the prices between them, the second run's are what the store holds, the facts file included (see: A re-run replaces a night's facts file where the store now computes a different one). It does not produce a byte-identical store, and is not meant to. Each row carries the instant it was observed, and the run log gains a row per stage per run, so the store also records that the night ran twice. That is the evidence the run page reads, and a run that left no trace of having repeated would be the thing to fix rather than the thing to claim. The schedule is a UTC instant after the provider posts the day's bulk file, moved later only by a night that finds the file not yet posted, and registered with whatever scheduler the machine has, because scheduling lives outside the application and a schedule written in local time moves twice a year with nothing to announce it (see: Queued work runs off-peak, and every schedule is written in UTC) (see: The night runs at a fixed UTC instant, moved only when a night finds the day's file not yet posted) (see: Nothing is written against one operating system).

15. The screens

This section is the specification for what the operator looks at. It is here rather than in a document of its own because a mockup file and a built page are two answers to one question, and the day they disagree nothing says which is the specification.

15.1 What these screens are for

A full report for one name is a long document. It is read in the evening by one person deciding which of a handful of names is worth the next half hour, and the sections that answer that question sit among sections that answer different ones. Every screen below replaces reading with looking. None of them replaces the prose: the researched sections are kept in full, one disclosure down, because the screen answers which name and the prose answers why. There are five: tonight at 15.7, the universe at 15.8, a name at 15.9, the run at 15.10 and the queue at 15.15. The queue is numbered after the sections that follow the other four rather than among them, because the numbers here are navigation and the record already cites 15.11 to 15.14.

15.2 The rule that governs every screen

Reads and renders, nothing else. No screen computes a level, calls a model, or makes a network request. Every number on every screen is already in the facts file, the ladder, the listings, the research store or the run log. A screen that wants a number the store does not carry is not a screen change; it is a change to a compute component, and it goes through a checkpoint like anything else.

The reason is that a rendering layer which computes is a second implementation of the same arithmetic, and two implementations of one rule disagree eventually. It also means a screen can be rebuilt or restyled at any time without a chance of moving a number. (see: A screen reads and renders, and computes nothing)

A figure is drawn at the places it is read at: a price, a ratio and an amount per share to two places, an amount of money and a count of shares in its scale, a fraction as a percentage to one place and a multiple to one. The element it is drawn in carries the stored value whole, and that is what the checks read, so rounding a figure to be read never makes a value the store does not hold. Prose a model wrote is drawn as the claim checker accepted it. (see: A figure is drawn at the places it is read at, and its element carries the stored value whole)

A screen is read at the width of the screen it is read on. The column every screen is drawn in is that width, up to a ceiling which is the widest picture a screen draws with the card's own padding and the page's gutter around it, so a wide screen is filled by the page rather than by the space beside it and a narrow one is filled rather than shown a page laid out for a wide one. A table cannot be narrowed past its columns, so every table a screen draws is read in a box of its own that scrolls sideways rather than moving the page. A picture is narrowed instead: the chart and the volume profile beside it are drawn at a size of their own, which is what the ceiling holds, and below it the pair is scaled down together so that a price is at one height in both at every width. (see: A screen is read at the width of the screen it is read on)

15.3 One document, no pages

One file, and the reader never loads a second one. Views are hash routes resolved in the browser: #/, #/night/<date>, #/watch, #/universe, #/researched, #/name/<ticker>, #/name/<ticker>/<date>, #/run/<date>. Back and forward work because the router listens for the hash changing and pushes no state the browser did not ask for. A route is a link, and the selected name is part of the route, so what is on screen can be sent to another machine and be the same thing.

No framework, no build step, no package manager. The nightly run already writes one store and the read API already serves it; adding a front-end toolchain would put a second source of formatted numbers into a project whose whole argument is that there is one. (see: One application, not a file per report)

15.6 Colour

The document you are reading and the application share one palette, in light and dark, with both designed rather than one inverted from the other. The theme is a stamp on the root element, remembered between visits, with the machine's own preference as the default.

Support and resistance own two hues, and nothing else uses them

Green means a level below the price, orange means a level above it, on every screen and in every mark. Nothing else is allowed those two hues, and in particular a day's price change is not: a green day and a support band would then be the same colour meaning two things, and a reader cannot hold that. Direction of the day is carried by the sign on the number and by which side of a zero rule a bar sits on. (see: Support and resistance own two hues and nothing else uses them)

Hue is never the only channel

Every band carries its role in words as well as its colour. Every reason carries its name. A reader who cannot separate the two hues loses nothing, which is a property to hold rather than to test for once: the two hues in the palette measure far enough apart under the commonest form of colour blindness to be told apart, and the words are there whether they do or not.

Magnitude is one ramp, never a rainbow

Where a mark needs to show more or less, it uses four steps of a single hue, stepped for the surface it sits on and validated against it. More is darker in light and lighter in dark. A second hue is never introduced to tell two bars apart: if a chart needs two hues to be readable it is the wrong chart.

Not yet measured is a dashed outline

Anywhere a figure has not earned a number yet, the space is drawn as a dashed outline carrying its count against its minimum, and never as a pale version of a value. The record column in 15.11 is in that state for the first year, and a pale number would read as a small one. (see: Not yet measured is drawn as a dashed outline, never as a pale value) (see: The record column stays empty until it has earned a number)

Each region is a card on one paper palette. 15.13 Routing and state

  • Hash routes only. No server-side routing, no history API, no build step.
  • A route is a link. The selected name and any filters live in the hash, so a filtered universe view and a selected candidate can both be sent to another machine.
  • Back and forward work, because the router listens for the hash changing and pushes nothing the browser did not ask for.
  • The theme is a stamp on the root element, remembered between visits, with the machine's preference as the default and both themes designed rather than one inverted.
  • An unknown route resolves to tonight with a line saying what was asked for, rather than to a blank page.
  • Any name is found from a search box in the masthead of every screen, by its ticker or its company's name, and a name found is opened on its own route. Text matching no name says so rather than opening an empty page.

20. Build phases, each with its visible output

Phase 1 carries a measurement that sets the ceiling on every later report, and it must not be replaced by an assumption. The belief that a large-company index guarantees good coverage is plainly true for the largest names and much weaker at the other end, where a mid-cap industrial may draw two or three articles a quarter, mostly wire copy and aggregator summaries. So phase 1 pulls two weeks of news across a sample of thirty names deliberately spread across the market-capitalisation range rather than chosen from the watch list, counts distinct publishers by frequency, and checks each for whether its text can actually be fetched. 21. Decisions

The decisions and the superseded ones live in the decisions record kept beside this document, grouped by topic, and not here. A decision stated in this document and again in a register is two places holding one fact, and one of them always loses.

Every rule in this document that a later session could reasonably choose differently, where the wrong choice would be invisible, is a named decision in that record. This document cites them by their exact name, in the form (see: Code owns every number), and the check named decision-resolves asserts that every citation matches a bold name in the record and that no two decisions share a name. The check named no-superseded-citation asserts that no citation resolves to a name under Previously decided.

Cite the name, never a number. A misremembered name fails to resolve; a misremembered number resolves to the wrong decision and nobody notices.
