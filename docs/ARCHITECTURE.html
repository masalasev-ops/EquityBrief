1. Purpose and boundaries

What it is for. To answer one question every evening, which stocks are sitting at a price their own chart has made significant, and then to produce a full research report on any name you choose to open. Four of the six conditions detect arrival at a price the evening's arithmetic has an instruction for, one detects a break on unusual volume, and one is a calendar fact. That arithmetic is not authored in advance and is not carried over from a previous night: the levels and the plan are recomputed from the chart every evening, so a name arriving at a level and the plan naming that level are the same night's work. Whether those arrivals are worth acting on is measured rather than assumed, and section 13 is how. The universe is the S&P 500, and its membership is fetched from the provider rather than maintained by hand. (see: The universe is the S&P 500, and membership is fetched, not maintained) The report it produces, specified in section 4, (see: Section 4 of the architecture defines the report, and nothing outside the corpus does) carries a narrative verdict, how the stock got here with the causes of its biggest moves, what the company sells, the numbers over five quarters, the industry cycle it sits in, the bull and bear cases side by side, a chart with levels and a volume profile, a staged entry and exit plan in two books, a risk table where every risk names the number that would confirm it, and a dated calendar.

The constraint that shapes everything. Nightly work must be free and fast, and paid work must happen only when you ask for it. So the nightly run is arithmetic over price bars with no model calls and no per-name network calls, and everything expensive happens when you open a name and is then stored permanently with the date it was written.

What it is not. It is not a screen: the universe is a published index membership, not a filtered set, and the shortlist selects on chart state alone with no fundamentals and no model in the decision. It does not trade, does not size positions, and produces no short or options ideas. (see: The plan places a position and never sizes one) (see: Improving what surfaces a name is in scope; ranking names against each other is not)

Who runs it. Program in .NET, with a local model on and an EODHD subscription.

2. The shape in one paragraph

After the US close, one bulk call fetches the day's bars for the whole exchange. For all 500 names the system computes indicators, swings, a volume profile, level bands, a trend label, an entry and exit ladder, and the reasons each name does or does not belong on tonight's list. That is seconds of arithmetic and it costs nothing. The result is stored. When you open the app you see tonight's list, built from those reasons. When you open a name, the computed sections of its report appear immediately from the store, its fundamentals fill from the provider, and if no research record exists for it a research pass runs once, reads filings and news, writes the narrative sections, and stores them with an as-of date and every source it read. Opening that name again shows the stored research with its date. A new pass runs only when a filing appears, an earnings date passes, the name's news feed moves materially, or you press refresh. (see: Research is a stored document with an as-of date, not a cache)

3. Vocabulary

Every specialist word used later in this document. Market words first, then the project's own words.

WordMeaning here
TickerThe short code for a listed company. MU is Micron Technology.
Daily barOne trading day as five numbers: opening price, highest price, lowest price, closing price, and shares traded. A candlestick chart draws one per day.
Moving averageThe average closing price over the last n days, recalculated daily. It smooths noise. Price above a rising average is the simplest definition of an uptrend.
Support, resistancePrices where the stock has repeatedly stopped falling, or stopped rising. They matter because many participants watch the same prices, so the behaviour repeats.
Swing high, swing lowA local peak or trough: a day whose high or low is more extreme than the days on either side of it.
BandA level given as a range rather than one price, because several reasons for a level usually cluster within a few points. The report writes "900 to 940" rather than claiming a precision it does not have.
Fibonacci retracementFixed fractions (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) of the distance between a swing high and a swing low. Widely watched, so bounces often stall there.
Volume profile, volume at priceHow many shares changed hands in each price band over a period. Where a lot traded, many holders have that price as their cost, so they defend it on the way down and sell at breakeven on the way up. Where little traded, price moves through quickly.
ATR, average true rangeHow far the stock moves on a normal day, averaged over 14 days. The report calls it "a typical day's move". It sets how far a stop must sit so ordinary noise does not trigger it.
RSIA momentum score from 0 to 100. Near 50 is balanced, above 70 stretched upward, below 30 stretched downward.
MACDThe gap between a fast and a slow average, with a signal line. A shrinking positive histogram means a rally is losing pace rather than reversing.
Trend stateA one-word label for the chart, uptrend, downtrend or range. It decides which ladder shape applies and how stops are placed.
TrancheOne step of a staged purchase. The plan buys at successively lower support bands rather than all at once.
StopThe price at which an entry is admitted to be wrong. This system uses daily-close stops: the exit triggers only if the day ends beyond the line, because intraday dips through a level are routine.
InvalidationThe single price below which the whole position, not just one tranche, is wrong.
Reward to riskDistance from entry to target divided by distance from entry to stop.
The print, guidanceThe quarterly results announcement, and the company's own forecast for the next quarter given with it. Stocks move most on those days.
Base rateWhat happens to an average name on an average night. Any claim that a signal predicts something must beat it, because most stocks rise over a month regardless.
Break-evenThe share of the time a plan must reach its target before its stop simply to come out level. It is arithmetic on the plan's own numbers: risk divided by risk plus reward. A plan with a distant target and a tight stop needs to be right rarely; one with a near target and a wide stop needs to be right often.
Computed sectionA part of the report produced by arithmetic over stored bars and provider data. No model is involved and every number is reproducible.
Researched sectionA part of the report produced by a model reading filings, news and theme material. Written once, stored with an as-of date, and never rewritten on a timer.
Facts fileThe JSON for one name on one night holding every number the computed sections may use, each with its source. It is what the model is allowed to see and what the fixture is diffed against.
Research recordThe stored output of one research pass for one name: the prose of each researched section, the as-of date, the model used, and every source document read.
Theme recordResearch about an industry rather than a company, for example memory-chip contract pricing. One record serves every name in that industry.
Tonight's listThe names where a stated condition fired tonight. It is not a ranking and it has no fixed length.
ReasonOne of the six stated conditions that can put a name on tonight's list.
ListingThe record that a named stock appeared on tonight's list, holding which reasons fired and the plan as it stood that night. One row per name per night it was listed, kept forever (see: Your own listing history is kept forever).
SetupOne listing considered as a prediction: an entry, a stop and a target that will eventually be right or wrong. A setup is resolved when the target is reached, the stop is closed through, or the time cap expires.
ShadowScored and stored exactly like a live thing, but shown nowhere and acting on nothing. A shadow condition accumulates a record of how it would have done, so a decision to adopt it later rests on evidence rather than on the idea.
FixtureA frozen, committed set of inputs with expected outputs beside them, so a run is checked by diff rather than by opinion.
HarnessThe one command that reads this document's tables, checks the code against them, runs the fixture, and reports every claim as PASS, FAIL or UNEXAMINED.

6. The layers, one at a time

6.1 Sources outside

Prices, index membership, company financials, the earnings calendar and news all come from EODHD, which is already subscribed. Bars arrive through the bulk end-of-day endpoint, which returns the whole exchange in one request, so the nightly fetch does not grow with the size of the universe. Index membership comes from the index constituents endpoint, which carries join and leave dates, so membership is fetched rather than maintained by hand (see: The universe is the S&P 500, and membership is fetched, not maintained). News arrives in one dated feed request for the whole exchange and is attributed to names in code rather than asked for per name (see: News arrives in one dated feed request and is attributed to names locally).

Filings come from the regulatory archive, which is free, needs no key, and returns reported financials as structured data. Three things are taken from it. Segment revenue and margin by business unit, which the business explainer in section 4 needs. Management's own guidance, published as the earnings press release exhibit to a results announcement rather than as structured data. And, where a company chooses to file one, the verbatim call transcript as a second exhibit to the same filing.

That third item is opportunistic and the design does not depend on it. Some companies file a transcript and most do not, so the two-cases section is written from the press release and the filings for most names and gains the analysts' questions for the few that file them. No transcript provider is required, and the report omits what it does not have rather than guessing. (see: Transcripts are opportunistic, never a dependency)

Two models are used for different jobs. A local model on the operator's GPU writes prose from numbers, which is a small job it does well and free. DeepSeek V4 does research, which is a large-context job a local model does badly. Its API accepts the OpenAI and Anthropic request formats, so the endpoint is configuration rather than a code path, and the report footer always says which model wrote what. (see: Two models for two jobs, and the research model is DeepSeek V4) (see: A research record is written and dated per section, not as a whole)

One consequence shapes the research runner. DeepSeek offers no server-side web search, so the model cannot go and find things; it can only read what it is handed through tool calls (see: The model never fetches; components fetch and hand it documents). For a per-name pass that costs nothing, because the documents it needs are filings and news the system already fetches. For a theme record it matters, because an industry's own pricing cycle is published by research firms rather than filed with a regulator, and that is section 22's second question.

Rates carry a peak and off-peak split, with peak at double. In Eastern time the peak windows fall late at night, so the market session and the hours just after the close are off-peak and interactive reading is never billed at the peak rate. Anything queued rather than interactive is scheduled into off-peak deliberately (see: Queued work runs off-peak, and every schedule is written in UTC).

6.2 Store owned

One SQLite file. One year of bars is kept per name and no more (see: One year of bars, and no more). One year for 500 names is about 10 MB, and the whole store including research is comfortably under a gigabyte, so it is copied into a fixture, diffed and backed up by hand (see: The whole system is a checkout and one database file). Section 16 lists the tables.

6.3 Compute deterministic, nightly

Eleven components, all pure functions of stored data. Given the same bars they produce the same facts file byte for byte, which is what makes the fixture diff possible. The four carrying the most logic are opened up in sections 8 to 11.

6.4 Research on demand, paid

A pass runs for one name when it is opened and no valid record exists. It reads that name's filings and recent news, reads or triggers its theme record, and writes the narrative sections. Everything it read is stored with the record. The claim checker then rejects numbers not present in the facts file and claims whose source is not in the store. What survives is written once and read from then on.

6.5 Serve pages

A local web application serves a read-only API over the store and hosts the single-page app. Reports are rendered in the browser from stored data rather than written to disk nightly, so a layout change costs nothing and nothing goes stale on disk. The exporter renders one name through the same code path into a self-contained file, because handing a single report to someone is a stated use. (see: A single report can still be exported as a self-contained file)

6.6 Check sign-off

The harness is what makes a phase finished. It has no opinion of its own; it reads the claims this document makes and tests them. Section 19.

8. What the three builders each do, and why there are three

All three run for every name in the index, every night. None of them is watch-list only, and none is triggered by opening a name. They are three stages of one chain, each answering a different question about a single stock.

Key. The dashed box is the whole nightly chain for one stock, repeated for every name in the index. Green boxes compute something that stays true until the chart changes. The orange box is the only one that asks a question about today. At the bottom, the blue-topped box on the left is what every name gets regardless, and the orange-topped box on the right is the small set you actually read.

The arrows run one way only. The shortlist cannot be built without the ladder, and the ladder cannot be built without the bands, which is why they are three components rather than one.

8.1 The worked example

One name's figures are used throughout this document to make rules concrete. They come from a year of that name's daily bars, a large-capitalisation semiconductor company trading around 933 at the time, and they are cited later wherever a rule needs a number to be legible. The worked example illustrates the rules; it never defines them. Where a rule and the worked example disagree, the rule is right and the example is restated.

The Level builder reads sixty sessions of that stock's bars and finds the prices that matter. In the worked example those are support bands at 900 to 940, 855 to 870, 818 to 830 and 738 to 770, and resistance at 996, at 1057, at 1144 to 1214, and at the all-time high. Its output is a list of price bands, each with a role and the evidence that makes it a band. It knows nothing about buying or selling.

The Ladder builder takes those bands and turns them into a plan. The first tranche sits on the 900 to 940 band and stops on a daily close below 855, which is the low edge of the band beneath it. The second sits on 855 to 870 and stops below 815. The third sits on 818 to 830 and stops below 737. Exits scale out from 1057 upward, and the band at 996 is listed but not traded because it is closer than two typical days' moves to the entry. None of this is about tonight; the plan holds for as long as the bands do.

The Shortlist builder is the only one of the three that asks about today. It takes the plan and tonight's close and tests six conditions. With the close at 933, the price is inside the first tranche zone, so the reason named at entry zone fires. The next earnings date is inside the twenty-session horizon, so earnings soon fires as well. Two reasons, so the name appears on tonight's list, and the list row shows both.

Now take a name whose close sits in the middle of its range, far from every band, with no earnings near and ordinary volume. Nothing fires and it does not appear on the list. Its bands and its plan were still computed and stored, so opening it deliberately still shows a complete chart, a level table and a ladder. That is the difference between the two boxes at the foot of the figure: the left one is produced for all five hundred names, the right one is the handful worth reading tonight.

8.2 Why three components rather than one

The dependency runs strictly one way, and each stage has a different failure mode. A wrong band produces a wrong plan for that name. A wrong plan produces a wrong list row. A wrong condition threshold produces a list of the wrong length while every band and plan underneath it stays correct, which is a completely different thing to diagnose.

Keeping them apart is also what makes the read and write matrix in section 16 able to say anything. Each builder declares what it may touch, so a defect in the ladder cannot corrupt the bands the chart draws from, and the fixture can catch each stage on its own rather than only catching the end of the chain.

Merging is what turns three prices a few points apart into one band, and half a typical day's move is the merge distance because two prices closer than that cannot be told apart by anyone trading them. Touches are why a band can cite specific dated lows inside it without those days being swings in their own right.

The volume shelf is the addition this version makes, and it is load bearing rather than decorative. Where a large share of a period's volume changed hands in a narrow price range, many holders have that price as their cost, so they defend it on the way down and sell at breakeven on the way up. Where very little traded, there is nobody with a reason to act and price moves through quickly. A level builder without a volume profile can state neither, and both statements belong in the report. (see: Levels come from four sources: swings, moving averages, retracements of the last two swings, and heavy volume shelves)

A heavy volume shelf creates a band of its own and also adds strength to one it coincides with. The two are different questions and this document answers both: the first step collects a shelf as a candidate, so a shelf with no swing, average or retracement near it is a band on volume alone, and the last step adds a point where a band holds one. The alternative, that a shelf only ranks, would make this three sources and a modifier rather than four, and it would leave the range where almost nothing traded as something the report cannot state. (see: A heavy volume shelf creates a band of its own and also strengthens one it coincides with)

The window for swings, retracements and the volume profile is the last sixty sessions, which is about a quarter of trading and is what the worked example uses. One window and not two: a shelf drawn from a different span would put a band on volume no other member of the level table can see, and its share of the period would be a share of a different period. (see: The volume profile accumulates over the same sixty sessions as the level window) The profile divides that window's own high and low into twenty bands, and each day's volume is spread across the bands its own range covers rather than placed at its close, because a day that opened at 300 and closed at 320 did not trade every share at 320. (see: The volume profile is twenty bands across the window's own range) A name with fewer than sixty stored sessions gets no profile at all rather than one over a shorter period, since a share of the period carries no denominator on the row it is written on. (see: A name with fewer than sixty sessions gets no volume profile) Moving averages are computed from the full stored year, so the 200-day average is a real computed number rather than one copied from a data page, and they are the stated exception because an average is a price rather than a share of a period.

The five retracements are drawn between the window's most recent swing high and its most recent swing low, in whichever order they occurred. A retracement is a partial reversal of a move and so needs the two ends of one, which is one high and one low; the last two swings of any kind can be two highs, and a fraction of the distance between two highs retraces nothing. A window holding no swing of one of the two kinds produces no retracements that night rather than retracements drawn from a substitute end. (see: A retracement is drawn between the last swing high and the last swing low)

One candidate is marked differently from the others. A short moving average follows the price by construction, so a band anchored on it sits near the price whatever the price does. Measured on the worked example's own bars, the close was within half a typical day's move of its own 20-day average on seventeen of the last thirty-one sessions. Such a band is still a level and still appears on the chart, because a reader wants to see where the average sits, but each band records whether anything other than a moving average anchors it. Section 10 uses that mark.

The trend state decides the stop, and the reason is in the literature rather than in taste. Kaminski and Lo, in the Journal of Financial Markets in 2014, show that a stop reduces expected return under a random walk and can add value where returns trend, so a single stop rule applied to both a ranging name and a trending name is wrong in one of the two cases. In a range the stop is the range floor, which is the low edge of the next support band beneath the tranche. In an uptrend it trails, to the higher of that band and the most recent swing low beneath the tranche. Taking the higher of the two rather than the swing low alone is what keeps the trailing half meaningful: read literally, trailing the last higher low puts the stop wherever that low happens to be, which on a name that has run a long way is far below the band beneath and is looser protection than the range rule gives, and a trailing stop that can sit below the range floor is not trailing anything. (see: The trailing stop is the higher of the band beneath and the last swing low)

Two details in the tranche rule exist for reasons worth stating. A band is eligible when its low edge is below the price, not when the whole band is, because the worked example's first tranche runs from 900 to 940 with the price at 933 inside it; a band you are already trading in is exactly the one the first tranche belongs on, and it keeps its full width because a fill can land anywhere in it. And a band anchored only by a moving average carries no tranche, because a short average tracks the price, so such a band would sit at the price about half the time and the first condition would fire on nothing having happened. The average stays on the chart; it just does not decide where to buy. (see: A moving average is a level on the chart and never an anchor for a tranche)

This rule set produces the worked example's plan exactly. Its three tranches are its three support bands, and its three stops are the low edges of the bands beneath them. Its skipped exit is the only resistance band within two typical days' moves of the entry.

13. How the picks improve

Measuring something is not the same as improving it, and until this section the document only measured. This is the loop. It is deliberately small: one score per condition, one register of candidates, and one column in a table that already exists. No new component runs nightly, no new store is needed beyond what the listings already hold, and none of it is built before the evidence exists to run it.

13.1 The score, and why it is not a return

Every listing already carries a plan, and a plan states an entry, a stop and a target. That makes each listing a falsifiable prediction with a bar it must clear, computed from its own arithmetic and nothing else.

Take the worked example's first tranche. Entry near 920, stop on a close below 855, first traded target at 1057. Risk is 65 points, reward is 137, so the setup breaks even if the target is reached before the stop about 32% of the time. That number is not a preference or a benchmark borrowed from elsewhere; it falls out of where the bands sit.

So the question asked of every condition is: across all the setups it produced, did the target come before the stop more often than the setups' own break-even required. That is one number, it is a count of stored rows, and it is meaningful in a way a forward return is not. Twenty-one-day returns mostly measure whether the market went up, which is why the run page shows them against the universe base rate. This score has no such problem, because the bar moves with the setup. (see: A condition is judged against the break-even its own plan demands)

Key. Green boxes are computed nightly from stored bars, and the grey box at the right of the row is the case that matters most: a setup that has done nothing yet is neither a win nor a loss. Counting it as either would flatter or punish a condition for a trade that has not happened. It is held open until the target hits, the stop hits, or the time cap expires, and a timed-out setup is reported separately and never as a win.

The bar in the second box is computed from the plan itself. A setup with a distant target and a tight stop needs to be right rarely; one with a near target and a wide stop needs to be right often. Comparing conditions on wins alone would reward the second kind for nothing, which is why the score is always relative to what each setup demanded. (see: The significance threshold is divided by the family size, and the divisor is shown)

13.3 The guardrails

The danger in a loop like this is not that it fails to find anything. It is that it finds something that is not there. Run five candidate conditions of no real value and the chance that at least one clears an ordinary significance test is about 23%; run twenty and it is 64%. Picking the best of twenty noise processes and shipping it is the standard way a self-improving system makes itself worse while appearing to learn.

13.4 What each phase owes

The loop is a phase 7 feature. But it cannot be added in phase 7 unless phase 5 stores the right things, because the evidence takes months to accumulate and cannot be recreated afterwards.

Phase 5 must record, for every listing, the plan as it stood that night: the entry zone, the stop and the first traded target. Storing only which reasons fired is not enough. Without the plan there is no break-even, and without the break-even none of the scoring in this section is possible. The bars can be replayed but the plan cannot, because by the time phase 7 arrives the rules may have changed and recomputing would score old listings under new rules. This is one JSON column written by a component that is already running, and it is the difference between phase 7 being a feature and phase 7 being a six-month wait.

Beyond that the phases carry what their table already says. Phase 5 also tunes the thresholds, because it is the phase that first records how many names fire each night. Phase 7 adds the scoring, the register, the shadow column and the run page's condition verdicts. Nothing else changes: no new nightly component, no new store, and no part of the reader's experience.

13.5 The boundary this must not cross

Improving which conditions surface a name for reading stays inside what section 1 says this tool is. Ranking names by expected return is a different tool with different obligations, and a scoring loop makes that line easy to cross one small step at a time. The test to apply to any future change: does it decide which chart is worth opening, or does it decide which stock is better than another. The first is this project. The second is not. (see: Improving what surfaces a name is in scope; ranking names against each other is not)

14. The nightly run, in order

Before the first nightly run, once

A single day of bars computes nothing: no averages, no swings, no bands, no ladder and no list. It is a few hundred requests in one afternoon and it never repeats for a name that already has its year. One year is what is kept and no more (see: One year of bars, and no more). The same backfill runs for a single name on the night it joins the index.

The computations from the indicators to the facts file were one step until 4.0, reading "For every name: indicators, swings, volume profile, levels, trend state, ladder, moves, list reasons, facts file". They are one step each because the night runs them stage by stage over the whole universe rather than name by name, which is what the components do, and because the order inside them is load bearing: the levels cannot be built before the swings and the ladder cannot be built before the levels. Written as one step the order was invisible and so was the fact that three of the stages, having shipped in phase 3, were not run by any night at all. That is the case for a claim per stage rather than one claim over all of them, and it is contradiction F's argument applied to a list instead of a row. It does not produce a byte-identical store, and is not meant to. Each row carries the instant it was observed, and the run log gains a row per stage per run, so the store also records that the night ran twice. That is the evidence the run page reads, and a run that left no trace of having repeated would be the thing to fix rather than the thing to claim. 15. The screens

This section is the specification for what the operator looks at. It is here rather than in a document of its own because a mockup file and a built page are two answers to one question, and the day they disagree nothing says which is the specification.

15.1 What these screens are for

A full report for one name is a long document. It is read in the evening by one person deciding which of a handful of names is worth the next half hour, and the sections that answer that question sit among sections that answer different ones. Every screen below replaces reading with looking. None of them replaces the prose: the researched sections are kept in full, one disclosure down, because the screen answers which name and the prose answers why.

15.2 The rule that governs every screen

Reads and renders, nothing else. No screen computes a level, calls a model, or makes a network request. Every number on every screen is already in the facts file, the ladder, the listings, the research store or the run log. A screen that wants a number the store does not carry is not a screen change; it is a change to a compute component, and it goes through a checkpoint like anything else.

The reason is that a rendering layer which computes is a second implementation of the same arithmetic, and two implementations of one rule disagree eventually. It also means a screen can be rebuilt or restyled at any time without a chance of moving a number. (see: A screen reads and renders, and computes nothing)

15.3 One document, no pages

One file, and the reader never loads a second one. Views are hash routes resolved in the browser: #/, #/universe, #/name/<ticker>, #/run/<date>. Back and forward work because the router listens for the hash changing and pushes no state the browser did not ask for. A route is a link, and the selected name is part of the route, so what is on screen can be sent to another machine and be the same thing.

No framework, no build step, no package manager. The nightly run already writes one store and the read API already serves it; adding a front-end toolchain would put a second source of formatted numbers into a project whose whole argument is that there is one. (see: One application, not a file per report)

15.6 Colour

The document you are reading and the application share one palette, in light and dark, with both designed rather than one inverted from the other. The theme is a stamp on the root element, remembered between visits, with the machine's own preference as the default.

Support and resistance own two hues, and nothing else uses them

Green means a level below the price, orange means a level above it, on every screen and in every mark. Nothing else is allowed those two hues, and in particular a day's price change is not: a green day and a support band would then be the same colour meaning two things, and a reader cannot hold that. Direction of the day is carried by the sign on the number and by which side of a zero rule a bar sits on. (see: Support and resistance own two hues and nothing else uses them)

Hue is never the only channel

Every band carries its role in words as well as its colour. Every reason carries its name. A reader who cannot separate the two hues loses nothing, which is a property to hold rather than to test for once: the two hues in the palette measure far enough apart under the commonest form of colour blindness to be told apart, and the words are there whether they do or not.

Magnitude is one ramp, never a rainbow

Where a mark needs to show more or less, it uses four steps of a single hue, stepped for the surface it sits on and validated against it. More is darker in light and lighter in dark. A second hue is never introduced to tell two bars apart: if a chart needs two hues to be readable it is the wrong chart.

Not yet measured is a dashed outline

Anywhere a figure has not earned a number yet, the space is drawn as a dashed outline carrying its count against its minimum, and never as a pale version of a value. The record column in 15.11 is in that state for the first year, and a pale number would read as a small one. (see: Not yet measured is drawn as a dashed outline, never as a pale value) (see: The record column stays empty until it has earned a number)

15.12 Opening a name, in order

  1. The computed sections render immediately from the nightly store.
  2. If the stored fundamentals predate the name's latest filing, they are fetched and the numbers section fills in.
  3. The staleness judge answers its four questions from stored data.
  4. Whatever sections are stored render with their own dates and the model that wrote each. A section drafted overnight by the local model says so, with the option to have the paid model rewrite it. Nothing is spent for any of this.
  5. Sections the local lane does not own are absent until asked for, which on the current machine means the two cases, the risks and the short version. Asking runs a paid pass for those sections only, with progress shown, and the sections stream in as they are checked and stored.

15.13 Routing and state

  • Hash routes only. No server-side routing, no history API, no build step.
  • A route is a link. The selected name and any filters live in the hash, so a filtered universe view and a selected candidate can both be sent to another machine.
  • Back and forward work, because the router listens for the hash changing and pushes nothing the browser did not ask for.
  • The theme is a stamp on the root element, remembered between visits, with the machine's preference as the default and both themes designed rather than one inverted.
  • An unknown route resolves to tonight with a line saying what was asked for, rather than to a blank page.

15.14 What no screen does

  • No screen fetches from a provider or calls a model. The only thing a screen can trigger is a research pass, and that is an explicit action with a visible cost, not a side effect of navigation.
  • No screen computes a level, a stop or a rate. Every figure is read, and every part of a page states where it came from and as of when. (see: A screen reads and renders, and computes nothing) (see: Every part of a page states where it came from and as of when)
  • No screen shows a figure without its denominator where the figure is a share.
  • No screen shows a number beside a ticker that could be read as that name's probability of rising.
  • No screen ranks names by anything other than distance to a level, which is a fact about the chart rather than a judgement about the company. (see: Improving what surfaces a name is in scope; ranking names against each other is not)

19. Fixtures and the verification harness

This document is the specification. 20. Build phases, each with its visible output

21. Decisions

The decisions and the superseded ones live in docs/DECISIONS.md, grouped by topic, and not here. A decision stated in this document and again in a register is two places holding one fact, and one of them always loses.

Every rule in this document that a later session could reasonably choose differently, where the wrong choice would be invisible, is a named decision in that record. This document cites them by their exact name, in the form (see: Code owns every number), and the check named decision-resolves asserts that every citation matches a bold name in the record and that no two decisions share a name. The check named no-superseded-citation asserts that no citation resolves to a name under Previously decided.

Cite the name, never a number. A misremembered name fails to resolve; a misremembered number resolves to the wrong decision and nobody notices.
